## A2. Which elements are protectable; look-and-feel law; "inspired by" vs "copy"

### Takeaway
The names and logos ("Grand Theft Auto", "GTA", "GTA VI", the "VI" logo) are trademarks and are the highest-risk items, followed by any copied artwork (HUD sprites, blip icons, map art, screenshots, music). A general style (dark radar circle, coloured route line, icon blips) is closer to unprotectable idea or functional convention. However, Tetris v. Xio (2012) shows US courts will protect a game's distinctive look and feel when the copying is near-verbatim.

### Cited Findings
- **Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive, 863 F. Supp. 2d 394 (D.N.J., 30 May 2012):**
  - The court granted summary judgment to Tetris on both copyright and trade dress claims. It held that the colours and styles of the pieces were "arbitrary flourishes," that is non-functional expression, and found Xio's game "nearly indistinguishable" in look and feel.
  - The trade dress claim was not preempted by copyright because it addressed consumer confusion.
  - A permanent injunction followed in 2013.
  - Sources: [Justia opinion](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-jersey/njdce/3:2009cv06115/235418/61); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2012/06/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc); [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2012/06/19/videogame-app-developer-breaks-the-rules-on-copyright-infringement/); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2013/02/12/tetris-gets-permanent-injunction-against-xio/); [Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc.)
- **Trademark as an anti-clone tool:** commentary notes trademark law is often stronger than copyright against copycat games. — [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/trademark-law-more-than-an-after-thought-in-video-game-anti-piracy-efforts.html)
- **Pricedown typeface:** Pricedown is a Typodermic (Ray Larabie) font. Licence information conflicts:
  - Font Squirrel hosts a Larabie freeware licence that allows commercial use. — [Font Squirrel](https://www.fontsquirrel.com/license/Pricedown)
  - Fontspace lists Pricedown as "Freeware, Non-Commercial," with a commercial licence to be purchased. — [Fontspace](https://www.fontspace.com/pricedown-font-f19482)
  - Commentary notes that licensing the font gives no rights to the GTA brand. — [Designbeep](https://designbeep.com/2025/12/09/grand-theft-auto-font/); [MadeGoodDesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/what-font-does-rockstar-use/)
- **Precedent for "inspired-by" game styling of real maps:** a public tutorial styles a Mapbox map after Red Dead Redemption 2, using Maki icons recoloured (black background, white glyph, round) rather than copied game sprites. — [Lee Martin, DEV](https://dev.to/leemartin/how-i-designed-a-red-dead-redemption-2-inspired-map-in-mapbox-studio-4gkh)

### Inferences
- **Don't:**
  - "GTA" / "GTA VI" / "Grand Theft Auto" in the app name, icon or store metadata
  - the GTA VI logo or the Rockstar "R" logo
  - ripped blip or HUD sprites, game map tiles, screenshots, trailer frames or in-game music
  - Leonida or Vice City map imagery
- **Risky:**
  - a near pixel-exact HUD clone, meaning the same radar frame geometry plus health/armour arcs plus the GTA VI colour palette and font, especially if marketed as "GTA VI map" (Tetris-style look-and-feel and trade-dress exposure, plus confusion)
  - using Pricedown or a near-identical font for the app title, given the licence conflict and brand association
  - descriptive "GTA-style" in marketing copy. In the US this is a possible nominative fair use, but it invites review rejection.
- **Safer:** original icons drawn from scratch; a generic circular radar, waypoint and route line (functional conventions shared by many games); your own palette; an original name. Phrases like "game-style" or "open-world-style" avoid any trademark.
- Jurisdiction: EU and Indian trademark law also protect well-known marks against unfair advantage. The US nominative fair use doctrine has no exact EU or Indian equivalent, so "GTA-inspired" wording is riskier outside the US.

### Gaps
- I found no case law specific to HUD/minimap designs.
- I did not verify Take-Two's registered trademark list (USPTO/EUIPO/India), for example whether a blip-style mark is registered.
- No case specifically on "styled after" real-world maps was found.

## A4. App store rules on third-party IP; does non-commercial status help?

### Takeaway
Both stores prohibit unauthorised third-party trademarks and copyrighted material and copycat metadata. Apple will ask for proof of permission when it notices third-party branding. Non-commercial status lowers Take-Two's practical incentive, since its own mod policy carves out non-commercial work. It gives no legal defence against trademark misuse, and app-store distribution with ads or IAP is commercial anyway.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 4.1 (Copycats):** "Don't simply copy the latest popular app… or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own." — [Apple App Review Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/4-1-prevent-copycat-rejection); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2554476)
- **Apple 5.2.1:** "Don't use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission, and don't include misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata in your app bundle or developer name." **[from prior knowledge; current text not fetched. Check developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.1]**
- **Google Play:** the Impersonation policy bans apps that misrepresent identity or ownership. A separate IP policy handles copyright and trademark complaints from rights holders, and Google recommends sending written proof of permission to use third-party brand names, logos or graphic assets before submission. — [Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374); [Play IP policy](https://play.google.com/intl/zh-CN/about/ip-impersonation/ip/); [GoodBarber on IP rejections](https://www.goodbarber.com/help/shop/google-rejection-r101/google-rejection-intellectual-property-infringement-a223)
- **Commercial vs non-commercial:** Rockstar's 2017 policy conditions tolerance on projects being "non-commercial." — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/after-angering-gta-5-fans-rockstar-releases-new-st/1100-6451151)

### Inferences
- The likeliest failure mode is app review rejection or a rights-holder complaint triggering removal, not a lawsuit. Keeping "GTA" out of the name, keywords, screenshots and description avoids this.
- Once ads, a paid tier or IAP are added, the 2017 "non-commercial" goodwill clearly no longer applies.

### Gaps
- I did not find a documented case of a GTA-referencing app removed from a store on IP grounds.

## A5. Map data licensing obligations

### Takeaway
Using OSM-derived tiles that you style yourself (OpenMapTiles, Protomaps, Overture's ODbL themes) is the cleanest route to a custom game look. The styled tiles are a "Produced Work": OSM attribution is required, but share-alike does not apply to the rendered map, only to any data you redistribute or derive. Google's terms effectively prohibit this kind of project outside its SDK. Mapbox allows full restyling but requires its logo plus attribution.

### Cited Findings
- **OSM / ODbL:**
  - Rendered maps, screenshots and tile services are "Produced Works" and can be under any licence. Distributed data and derivative databases must stay ODbL.
  - Recipients may request the underlying data.
  - Using a produced work to extract or recreate substantial data creates a derivative database.
  - Attribution must be "reasonably calculated" to make viewers aware, should not require interaction to see, and uses the credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors."
  - Sources: [OSMF Licence & Legal FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ); [OSMF Attribution Guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Attribution); [OSM wiki Legal FAQ](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Legal_FAQ); [OSM use cases](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/License/Use_Cases)
  - Commentary disputes the guideline's "public only" framing. — [imagico diary](https://www.openstreetmap.org/user/imagico/diary/397033)
- **OSM's own tile servers** are subject to a Tile Usage Policy, so an app should not use them as a production backend. — [OSMF FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ)
- **Protomaps:** the code is BSD-3, and the tilesets are Produced Works of OSM under ODbL. Attribution to Protomaps is requested, not required. Forks or tile APIs must use a different name than "Protomaps." — [Protomaps basemaps](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads); [Protomaps styles](https://github.com/protomaps/styles); [Geocode Earth, 14 Jul 2026](https://geocode.earth/blog/2026/protomaps-a-map-you-can-actually-own/)
- **Overture Maps:**
  - Overture prefers CDLA-Permissive-2.0, but OSM-derived themes are ODbL derivative databases, so users should follow OSMF guidance. — [Overture FAQ](https://overturemaps.org/about/faq/); [AWS Open Data Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/overture/)
  - A community diary claims Overture does not require attribution for CDLA themes; this is a third-party reading. — [OSM diary](https://www.openstreetmap.org/user/jacobwhall/diary/406979)
- **Mapbox:** requires both a Mapbox logo and text attribution. If you use neither Mapbox styles nor Mapbox data, text attribution can be omitted, but the logo must stay, and the logo may not be styled. — [Mapbox attribution docs](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/attribution/); [Mapbox FAQ](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/faq/what-type-of-attribution-is-required); [Mapbox ToS](https://www.mapbox.com/tos)
- **Google Maps Platform:**
  - Maps content may not be exported, extracted or scraped for use outside the Services, including bulk tile downloads.
  - Caching is allowed only where the terms expressly permit it.
  - Creating content based on Google Maps Content is prohibited.
  - Some SDK content may not be used with a non-Google map.
  - Sources: [Map Tiles API policies](https://developers.google.cn/maps/documentation/tile/policies?hl=en); [Maps Service Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms/?no_redirect=true); [Archived terms, 15 Oct 2026](https://cloud.google.com/archive/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms-20261015?hl=it)

### Inferences
- **Recommended stack:**
  - self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles or OpenMapTiles, rendered with MapLibre GL using your own game-style JSON style
  - a visible "© OpenStreetMap contributors" credit, which can be styled to fit the HUD as long as it stays visible without interaction
  - no Google tiles
- Mapbox is viable, but the unstyleable Mapbox logo will sit in the "game" HUD.
- Share-alike is only triggered if you publish or share a modified POI database. User-added campus POIs merged with OSM data would likely make a derivative database.

- I did not fetch Apple MapKit / MapKit JS terms. MapKit allows only limited styling (map types and overlays), and the Apple Maps logo and legal link are required **[from prior knowledge, unverified]**.
- I did not verify the OpenMapTiles schema licence. It is reported as CC-BY 4.0, requiring "© OpenMapTiles" attribution **[from prior knowledge, unverified]**.

## A6. Privacy basics if handling students' live location

### Takeaway
Precise location of minors is regulated everywhere. US COPPA (under-13s) treats geolocation as personal information and its 2025 amendments are now in force. India's DPDP Rules require verifiable parental consent for under-18s and ban tracking children, with a narrow safety carve-out. GDPR requires parental consent below 13–16 depending on the member state. FERPA only applies to schools and their contractors.

### Cited Findings
- **COPPA:**
  - The amended rule was published 22 Apr 2025, took effect 23 Jun 2025, with compliance required by 22 Apr 2026.
  - Geolocation is personal information (since 2013).
  - The amendments add a written data security programme and prohibit indefinite retention.
  - Sources: [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule); [BBB Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/coppa-amended)
- **India DPDP Rules (notified Nov 2025):**
  - Verifiable parental consent is required for anyone under 18.
  - Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising aimed at children are prohibited.
  - Real-time location strictly for the child's safety is exempt, and educational institutions are exempt under conditions.
  - Rule 10 obligations are reportedly enforceable from about 13 May 2027.
  - Sources: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified-309928); [DSCI](https://www.dsci.in/article/content/indias-framework-processing-childrens-data-and-disability-related-information); [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/india-notifies-its-sweeping-digital-personal-data-protection-rules); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)
- **GDPR Art. 8:** parental consent is required below 16, and member states may lower this to 13. **[from prior knowledge; not sourced in this session]**
- **FERPA:** applies to education records held by schools that receive federal funding and their vendors. It is relevant only if a university partners with the app or supplies data. **[from prior knowledge; not sourced]**

### Inferences
- Design defaults to reduce exposure: 18+ only (or university-email-verified adults) at launch, location processed on-device, friend-location sharing opt-in and ephemeral, and no ad-tech SDKs.

### Gaps
- GDPR and FERPA are not source-verified in this session.

## B1. What map-based apps students use; successes and failures

### Takeaway
Friend-location maps are the proven mass-market student behaviour: Zenly reached 35–40M MAU before Snap killed it in Feb 2023, and Snap Map claims 300M+ MAU. Campus navigation and orientation is a recurring, well-documented need, but it is fragmented and university-specific.

### Cited Findings
- **Zenly:**
  - Shut down 3 Feb 2023. It had about 35M MAU (some reports say about 40M) in 2022.
  - Snap shut it rather than sell it, to "focus on Snap Map," which claims 300M+ MAU (unclear how many use the map features).
  - Sources: [TechCrunch, Sep 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/01/zenly-is-still-hugely-popular-so-whys-snap-shutting-it-down); [TechCrunch, Dec 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/05/zenly-was-the-best-social-app-and-it-will-sadly-shut-down-on-february-3rd/); [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2022/fearing-competition-snap-decided-to-shut-down-zenly-rather-than-sell-it/?utm-source=sharing); [Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/zenly/)
  - Zenly co-founder Antoine Martin launched a new social-app company, Amo (Feb 2023), which built **Bump**. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/06/zenly-co-founder-returns-with-new-social-app-company-amo)
- **New students' needs:** surveys and focus groups show new students "feel lost, have trouble meeting new friends and finding what services and events are available on campus." — [QUT Orientation Passport paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/10907816.pdf)

### Inferences
- The social "where are my friends / where's the party" map is the behaviour with proven pull. The Zenly vacuum was partly filled by Snap Map, Life360 and Bump. Competing head-on is hard. A game-style skin with "squad blips" and shared waypoints is a plausible niche angle.

### Gaps
- No usage numbers were found for Bump, Life360's student share, study-spot finders, campus safety apps, food/discount maps or timetable-to-route apps.

## B2. Gamified real-world map products and engagement evidence

### Takeaway
Pokémon GO is the definitive proof that location-based gamified exploration can retain users for a decade: about 20M weekly players in 2025, over $1B revenue in 2025, 800M lifetime players. Evidence for smaller exploration apps is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- **Pokémon GO:**
  - 20M weekly active players and about 10M daily as of Mar 2025 (Niantic).
  - Over $1B revenue in 2025 and 800M lifetime players (Scopely, 10th anniversary, Jul 2026).
  - Niantic's games division was sold to Scopely for $3.5B (closed around May–June 2025).
  - Sources: [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-go-has-20m-weekly-active-players-nearly-nine-years-after-its-global-launch); [PocketGamer.biz, 10 years](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-marks-10-years-with-800m-players-and-a-trillion-pokemon-caught/); [PocketGamer.biz revenue](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-celebrates-10th-anniversary-with-over-9bn-in-revenue/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/pokemon-go-studio-niantic-sold-scopely-1236161480/)
- Third-party MAU estimates conflict (60M–125M) and should not be relied on. — [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/pokemon/how-many-people-play-pokemon-go-pokemon-go-player-count-2132719/)

### Inferences
- Fog-of-war "explore your city" mechanics (Fog of World, Wandrer, CityStrides) are a natural fit for a game-styled map. No usage data was found for them, so treat them as niche.

### Gaps
- No figures were found for Fog of World, Wandrer, CityStrides, Geocaching, Swarm, Strava Local Legends or Zombies, Run!.

## B4. Do game-style features drive engagement in orientation/education?

### Takeaway
The evidence is modest and mixed. Gamified orientation apps are well liked and somewhat raise engagement with orientation activities. Effects on user experience are weak, cheating is a risk, and at least one deployment (Técnico GO!) missed its goals.

### Cited Findings
- **QUT "Orientation Passport":** achievements were well received and had some effect on engagement with orientation activities, but no evident effect on user experience. Cheating occurred. — [QUT paper (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/10907816.pdf); [Fitz-Walter et al.](https://es.scribd.com/document/81579000/p122-Fitz-Walter)
- **Malaysian needs analysis:** students wanted navigation help plus gamification elements "to keep them interested." — [iJIM](https://online-journals.org/index.php/i-jim/article/view/24523)
- **University of Auckland online orientation:** interactive campus maps and gamified elements in each module were described as the key driver of success. — [JANZSSA](https://janzssa.scholasticahq.com/article/1342-interactive-online-student-transition-to-university)
- **START onboarding app** (thesis, Technische Hochschule Augsburg): most students responded more positively to the gamified version. — [THA poster](https://www.tha.de/homes/john/mobile-experience/Keller/MA-Poster_Gamification-of-University-Onboarding.pdf)
- **Técnico GO! (Lisbon):** gamification "as currently deployed, do[es] not achieve the expected goals." — [Springer, Multimedia Tools & Applications](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-023-16320-x)

### Inferences
- Candidate features, ranked by evidence plus fit:
  1. Fog-of-war campus discovery for freshers and orientation week, with "missions" such as finding the library, registrar or canteen. This is directly supported by the orientation literature.
  2. Class timetable turned into waypoint and route-line "missions" with walking ETAs.
  3. Campus POIs as blips: study spots, water, printers, food deals, events.
  4. Opt-in squad waypoints and meetups, filling the post-Zenly niche.
  5. Achievements and streaks, with anti-cheat (geofence check-ins) given QUT's cheating finding.

### Gaps
- No large controlled trials were found.

## B5. Data sources for campus maps and student POIs

### Takeaway
Outdoor campus data is generally good in OSM. Indoor data is patchy and needs university cooperation, either through Apple's IMDF programme or university OSM imports such as TUM's NavigaTUM.

### Cited Findings
- **IMDF:**
  - A GeoJSON (RFC 7946) indoor model, adopted as an OGC Community Standard (v1.0.0).
  - Apple's Indoor Maps Program lists universities as eligible venues, but the data must come from the venue owner via Apple Business Register.
  - Sources: [Apple IMDF](https://register.apple.com/resources/imdf/); [OGC](https://www.ogc.org/standards/indoor-mapping-data-format/); [Apple Indoor Maps Program PDF](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/Apple-Indoor-Maps-Program.pdf)
- **OSM:**
  - TUM's NavigaTUM indoor dataset was offered for import into OSM under ODbL (in forum review as of Jun 2025). — [OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Import/Indoor_Navigatum)
  - Other university projects include TU Dresden's campus navigator and Klagenfurt's indoor routing API. — [OSM wiki Indoor/Projects](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Indoor/Projects)
  - The community notes that indoor mapping is "badly supported by tooling." — [OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Import/Indoor_Navigatum)
- **Overture Places:** a CDLA-licensed places theme, a likely POI source alongside OSM. — [Overture FAQ](https://overturemaps.org/about/faq/)

### Gaps
- I did not survey university open-data portals or Indian campus OSM coverage.
